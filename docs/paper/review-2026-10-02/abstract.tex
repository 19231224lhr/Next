\begin{abstract}
Continuous payments let a recipient spend an output before its creating transaction executes publicly. In the Next UTXO ledger, a recipient verifies a quorum-signed output certificate (TXCer) after one certification round and can then request a successor, while replication and public execution proceed in the background. If the successor executes first, the committee consumes the missing input and records a direct obligation against its issuer. The issuer's original signers can resubmit the source from the certificate that the successor's block publishes. If the source is still absent at its deadline, a public decision debits the issuer's reserve, and a late source repays it. Restricted chameleon-hash commands later record the debit in the stored block, preserving owner authorizations, successor references, and the complete BlockID; economic closure does not wait for them. For four-member organizations and a four-validator committee, each requiring three votes and containing at most one static Byzantine member, we prove unique consumption, funding of every certificate's outstanding CAL liability, and delay-neutral terminal CAL holdings for source-closed, conflict-free payment sets. On one host with synchronous member commits, a 100-hop chain reaches its last certified receipt in a median 4.557\,s, versus 64.476\,s with per-hop waiting.
\end{abstract}
