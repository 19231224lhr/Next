\begin{abstract}
Continuous payments require recipients to reuse incoming value while
earlier transactions remain pending. Next supports this in an unspent transaction output (UTXO) ledger through registered guarantor
organizations. After verifying and recording an output and its
quorum-signed certificate (TXCer), a recipient can request a successor
while public execution proceeds asynchronously. If a certified source is
missing, the committee atomically executes the successor and records an
obligation backed by the issuer's reserve; the obligation closes when the
source executes or, after expiry, through a reserve debit, and a
source arriving after compensation repays the compensating issuer. Under
fixed membership and Byzantine faults we prove consumption uniqueness,
coverage of liabilities including hidden certificates, and delay
neutrality: for finite sets of non-conflicting payments whose sources all
execute, every successful arrival and compensation order ends with the
same final CAL holders and reserve balances as undelayed execution.
Restricted funding revisions preserve complete block identities. On one
M4 Max host (nine services, in-memory state, NoSync wallets), 100-hop
chains reach their last certified receipt in a median 158.846\,ms versus
65.204\,s with inter-hop public-confirmation waits, and three
30,000-payment runs complete 1,750--1,887 payments/s including drain.
Nine recovery runs (disk-backed committee) close 21,600 payments and
repay all 216 compensations.
\end{abstract}
