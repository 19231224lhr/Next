\section{Implementation and Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

We evaluate certified continuation, source resolution during ordinary
traffic, and local historical reading on frozen evaluation build
\texttt{721800c}. It integrates independent compensation decisions,
public-certificate source recovery, and one-to-one input-certificate
checks. The two network experiments use identical node binaries and
Comet sources; additions are confined to observation and offline test
tools. Artifacts retain source/overlay/binary fingerprints and per-run records.
The local invalidation transition was added in patch \texttt{bf71c4d};
its targeted tests and short functional regressions are reported separately
in the supplement. The measurements below retain their original build
and do not measure that transition.

\noindent\textbf{Setup and measurement.}
Experiments use one Mac Studio M4 Max with 16 CPU cores, 64\,GiB RAM,
macOS~26.6.2, and Go~1.27.1. The project overlay of CometBFT~v0.38.26
implements the identity and installation rules. Network trials use
synchronous bbolt member commits before signing; committee application
state, BlockStore, and gateways are disk-backed. Members use
\texttt{GOMAXPROCS=16} and \texttt{GOGC=200}; commit, flush, and gossip
settings are 250, 10, and 10\,ms. Network trials copy the same unrun genesis and keys, reset genesis time,
and use sufficient fixed funding without top-up.

Certified receipt runs from first actual send to recipient verification
and atomic local recording. Public and member observations include
block following and driver polling; the chain experiment separately records
application-commit completion. Unless stated otherwise, summaries take
the median of three run-level statistics, not pooled transaction
quantiles; ranges describe runs, not confidence intervals. These finite single-host trials do not measure crash recovery,
sustainable throughput, or minimum capital.

\subsection{Continuous Spending Without Per-Hop Settlement}
\label{sec:eval-continuation}

Nine services support three independent NoSync wallets rotating
100\,CAL along $A\to B\to C\to A$, with each payment using the owner's
final FUEL. After a uniform three-second warm-up, successors are
constructed online from preceding recipients' stored outputs.
Fast mode proceeds after certified receipt; wait mode additionally
awaits the driver's observation of wallet-verified public execution.
Three pairs run in fast/wait, wait/fast, fast/wait order.

Both modes measure $\mathsf{Sent}_1\to\mathsf{Ready}_{100}$, including
construction of hops 2--100 and retries, but excluding first-payment
construction. Wait mode adds 99 inter-hop waits, not final-payment public
confirmation. Median chain time is 4.557\,s versus 64.476\,s, a
14.15-fold ratio and 92.93\% reduction
(Fig.~\ref{fig:continuation}); ranges are 4.498--4.740 and
64.461--64.627\,s. Fast uses 300 attempts for 300 payments; wait uses
407 attempts. The comparison includes retry costs without attributing
every retry or the entire difference to consensus waiting.

All 297 fast successors use their parent's TXCer, are constructed after
parent receipt, and are first sent before the earliest parent
application-commit completion recorded across all four replicas.
The minimum lead is 224.785\,ms. These records timestamp database commit, not consensus decisions.
Later driver observations are not original wallet-validation timestamps. Actual missing-input records, rather than send
ordering, establish 3, 2, and 4 child-before-source executions, all
fulfilled without compensation.

Fast medians are 5.228\,s for last-payment public observation,
5.229\,s for all-payment public observation, and 5.294\,s for all-payment
member closure. All 600 payments pass input/output, replica-agreement,
CAL/FUEL, fee-closure, and outbox audits. Each chain spends 9,400\,FUEL:
8,400 rewards and 1,000 burned. For this fixed shape, TXCer size is
779\,B; requests are 1,979\,B initially and 2,766\,B thereafter,
without ancestry-dependent growth.

\subsection{Source Resolution Under Mixed Load}
\label{sec:eval-mixed}
\label{sec:eval-liabilities}
\label{sec:eval-repair}

Four committee processes and two organizations, each with four members
and one gateway, give 14 services. Fees use explicitly authorized
organization-funded FUEL. Ordinary wallets are NoSync; scenario wallets
commit synchronously and follow blocks for at least 55\,s in every
treatment. Each run offers 7,000 independent ordinary payments targeting
100 payments/s for 70\,s, with limits of 64 fast requests and 256
outstanding payments. Starting ten seconds after driver launch, eight
independent cross-organization parent--child pairs start one second
apart, using inputs and Intents separate from the ordinary stream.
Every treatment includes these pairs.

N delivers normally. R withholds publisher submission and INSTALL of
each certified parent, leaving public-evidence recovery enabled.
C uses the same withholding with recovery disabled. P additionally
pauses all adaptation endpoints, but not consensus or decisions.
C/P release each late parent after its compensation is observed,
without awaiting another target or representation. P resumes adaptation
only after all four replicas report all eight targets Recovered with
representations neither committed nor materialized. Each treatment has
three fresh runs, ordered N/R/C/P, P/C/R/N, N/R/C/P.

\noindent\textbf{Economic closure while adaptation is unavailable.}
R fulfills all 24 withheld sources automatically, without publisher
resubmission or compensation. Across these targets, source execution is observed
2.054--3.357\,s after child send (median 2.905\,s).
C/P compensate 48 targets by 100\,CAL each and recover every debit:
4,800\,CAL paid and repaid. Across the 48 targets, compensation observations span
31.806--34.499\,s after child send, including the 30-second public-time
deadline, scheduling, execution, and polling.

In P, all 96 replica--target checks confirm repayment before adaptation
resumes, at 51.256--51.792\,s after the first ordinary send.
Every subsequent representation and materialization completes before
the last ordinary send. This ordering demonstrates economic closure
without available adaptation in the measured executions
(Fig.~\ref{fig:mixed-load}), rather than inferring independence from
matching final balances.

\noindent\textbf{Shared-resource costs and audit.}
Table~\ref{tab:eval-mixed} reports admitted receipt latency alongside
actual send spans and drain. All N/C runs and two P runs reach the
256-outstanding limit; two C runs and two P runs also reach 64 fast requests.
Dispatch lag, measured from scheduled to actual send, reaches a
run-level P95 of 895.88\,ms. The P run-1 tail after 70\,s contains
89 payments with receipt P95 282.61\,ms; it is not a full ten-second
window. Thus lower aggregate quantiles do not establish a performance
improvement or zero interference. All runs drain 0.815--1.356\,s after
the last ordinary send.

All 84,192 payments across the twelve runs close. Each run's four replicas agree at the same
height; CAL/FUEL conservation, fees, identities, obligations, and
outboxes pass audit. Final user CAL allocations coincide across
treatments, reserves recover, and CAL/work reservations clear without
budget rejection. N/R spend 659,504\,FUEL per run; C/P spend 659,544,
including eight additional 5-FUEL compensation fees. Actual fee
expenditure is not restored as available funding.

\subsection{Historical Reading: Equivalent Answers and Explicit Costs}
\label{sec:eval-reader}

The reader is an honest local replica that has verified and executed
prefix $H$, querying original coordinates $(h,j,k)$ within one
application read transaction $V_H$. A loads original history and uses
the permanent decision index; B uses existing Canonical revisions.
Both consult the same permanent decisions and obligations, returning
identical input identity, TxID, coordinates, amount, historical
funding/debit, decision/height, and obligation state at $H$.
Authorization comes from successful execution binding exact revisions,
not a CH equation or matching BlockID. This is not remote-server
authentication.

Fixtures use real signatures, successful ABCI execution, disk-backed
bbolt/GoLevelDB, and frozen BlockStores. A has its own original
BlockStore, without B's backup-layout cost. Point queries load a block
and decode the selected transaction; A's block query scans its range
index once and builds a timed map. Neither strategy receives a
precomputed decision map. Pages are warm, with no decoded-block or
result cache; \texttt{GOMAXPROCS=16}, \texttt{GOGC=100}.

Three fresh processes each test four $(n,m)$ fixtures, with
$n\in\{32,256\}$ payments and $m\in\{1,8\}$ compensated slots per block.
After 20 warm-ups per strategy, 300 interleaved queries per strategy
for compensated points, ordinary points, and whole blocks yield
21,600 timed queries. Timing includes read-transaction boundaries,
loading/decoding, record association, and result construction.
All timed reads use represented, materialized $H=4$; other stages
and subsequent real repayments are functional-equivalence checks,
not extra timing samples.

Canonical reduces compensated-point P50 by 7.44--12.32\%
(12.6--123.8\,$\mu$s); whole-block differences are 1.49--3.88\%
(Table~\ref{tab:eval-reader}). In $(256,8)$ run 1, A performs 12
BlockStore Gets returning 622,183\,B; B performs none but reads
825,526\,B from application state. Mean per-query heap allocation is approximately
4.95 versus 2.24\,MB, while record-association medians are
11.50 versus 10.83\,$\mu$s. This indicates a layout/reconstruction
benefit, not fewer total bytes or cryptographic acceleration.
Decoded-object caching could change that tradeoff.

From already decoded $(256,8)$ run-1 objects, separate authorization
rechecks cost about 26.3\,ms per block on both paths, input-CH
checks 10.3\,ms, and complete BlockID reconstruction 0.67\,ms.
These independent rechecks are excluded from trusted-reader timings.

Representation adds 0.28--2.19\,MiB of net logical KV per repaired
block, for revisions, tasks, and backup/history records, net of deleted Pending. The $(256,8)$ 5,056-B batch is already
included. Local sequential adaptation, assembly, Finalize, synchronous
application Commit, and materialization total 43--214\,ms.
It excludes network/consensus waits, public-block saving, and
commit-signature construction. Common compensation costs are reported separately.

Equivalence checks cover compensation before representation,
representation before materialization, and repayment preserving the
historical debit with state Recovered. Invalid coordinates,
cross-prefix decisions, and wrong debit/amount are rejected;
undecodable revisions fail rather than fall back. These checks do
not establish detection of arbitrary decodable corruption.
Canonical offers authorized funding representation at stable
historical coordinates, with measured maintenance costs, rather
than an economically necessary or universally faster alternative
to indexed history.
