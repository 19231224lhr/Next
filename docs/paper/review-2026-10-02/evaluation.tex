\section{Implementation and Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

We evaluate dependent-payment latency, reserve closure and repayment,
evidence-driven source resolution, and the cost of historical representation.
Organization-path and load experiments then characterize the operating
conditions of the fast path.

\noindent\textbf{Implementation and builds.}
The Go implementation uses atomic state overlays, independent block
followers, and CometBFT v0.38.26 modified to enforce complete block
identities and authorized historical installation. Threshold RSA adaptation
uses CIRCL v1.6.5 with trusted offline dealer initialization. Experiments run
on one Mac Studio (M4 Max, 16 CPU cores, 64\,GiB RAM, macOS~26.6.2,
Go~1.27.1). Four committee processes plus, per organization, four members and
one gateway give nine or fourteen service processes on this host, and every
run starts from fresh genesis.

Results come from three kinds of build, identified in the supplement. The
\emph{baseline build} (commit \texttt{d99ec92}) implements the layered
continuation and the accounting of Section~\ref{sec:protocol-execution}; its
compensation commits together with its representation. The
\emph{decision-first build} implements resubmission and the separate
compensation decision of Sections~\ref{sec:protocol-resubmission}
and~\ref{sec:protocol-decision}. \emph{Archived builds} supply the
delivery-gate, organization-path, operating-range, capital, and
batch-integration series and are labeled where used. A number measured on one
build is not restated for another.

\noindent\textbf{Measurement conventions.}
Certified-receipt latency starts at the first wallet HTTP send and ends after
recipient verification and atomic local recording. Public and member
completion times are client observations of authenticated execution that
include polling, not internal commit timestamps, and completion throughput
includes the final background drain. Latency and capacity trials mainly use
in-memory member and committee backends and bbolt wallets with NoSync, with
signatures and atomic state updates enabled; recovery and decision-first
trials use a disk-backed committee. Continuation and recovery trials use final
user FUEL, while storage, paired-background, and short-throughput controls use
authorized organization-paid FUEL.

\subsection{Continuous Spending Without Per-Hop Settlement}
\label{sec:eval-continuation}

We compare certified continuation with waiting for public execution between
hops. On the baseline build, a
100-hop chain reaches its last certified receipt in a median 158.846\,ms,
compared with 65.204\,s when each hop waits for public confirmation. Three
wallets transfer 100 CAL in rotation, constructing each payment online from
its predecessor's output and paying fees from their own final FUEL. Three
repetitions per mode give six runs and 600 payments on the nine-service
in-memory configuration. Both modes end at the last certified receipt, and
the waiting mode adds a public confirmation \emph{between} hops. With
committee commit, flush, and gossip settings of 250, 10, and 10\,ms, public
execution and its observation cost about 0.65\,s per hop. Certified
continuation removes that wait from the recipient's path.

Figure~\ref{fig:continuation} shows the six whole-chain durations. The fast
runs take 158.492--159.905\,ms. All 297 fast-mode successor requests consume
the preceding certified output and are sent after its certified receipt but
before its public completion is observed. A one-input, one-output TXCer is
779 bytes, and requests are 1,979 bytes at the first hop and 2,766 bytes
thereafter. For this transaction shape, the direct evidence stays the same
size across all 100 hops, because each request carries the certificates of its
direct inputs rather than the ancestor chain.

To test whether the advantage depends on in-memory member storage, three paired 100-hop trials on a nine-service disk-backed
configuration with NoSync wallets and user-paid FUEL differ only in member
storage (NoSync or synchronous; order N/S, S/N, N/S), and each is followed by
a synchronous waiting chain. Median durations are 681.127\,ms for NoSync
continuation, 4.471\,s for synchronous continuation, and 65.013\,s for
synchronous waiting. Certified continuation therefore remains 14.5 times
faster than waiting when member commits are synchronous. All 900 payments
complete with agreeing committee states.

On an archived delivery-gate build, a gate after certification waits for three
members to INSTALL complete material or for authenticated public success.
The gate collects no second signature quorum, and delivering immediately
instead shortens continuation. At 200
payments/s for 30\,s, three paired runs per mode each complete 6,000
payments, and the median of run-level receipt P50s falls from 2.167\,ms with
the gate to 1.300\,ms (40.0\%). Separate 100-hop trials fall from 287.983 to
200.356\,ms (30.43\%). Authorization is unchanged, and the supplement lists
the paired runs.

\subsection{Funded Closure and Repayment}
\label{sec:eval-liabilities}

This experiment checks that compensation leaves successor payments in place
and that each compensation is repaid exactly once when its source later
executes.

\noindent\textbf{Compensation and repayment matrix (baseline build).}
Nine runs use seeds 23, 37, and 59 at withheld-parent rates of 0\%, 1\%, and
5\%. Each run offers 20 two-hop units/s for 60\,s (1,200 units, 2,400
payments) against a fixed 60,000-CAL reserve with user-paid FUEL and no
top-up. The nine-service deployment uses disk-backed committee application
state and BlockStore, in-memory members and gateway, and NoSync wallets. The
driver releases each withheld parent only after its compensation command is committed
and its historical revision is materialized on all four replicas, so every withheld source is compensated
after the 30-s public-time deadline and executes afterwards.

All 21,600 payments close. The 216 compensations (12 per 1\% run, 60 per 5\%
run) are each followed by repayment. At the end of every run no obligation is
Open or Repaired, the gap is zero, net Spent and Reserved are zero, and the
reserve is back at 60,000\,CAL (Table~\ref{tab:eval-audit}). The lowest
sampled reserve is 59,800--59,900\,CAL at 1\% and 59,600\,CAL at 5\%. It
reflects the driver's release schedule rather than a reserve-sizing result,
and the supplement plots the three 5\% trajectories. Run-level receipt P50s
for normal child payments are 1.185--1.251\,ms. Measured from the deadline, the
committee commit, which in this build carries the representation, is observed
at a median of 1.003--1.254\,s, and physical installation of the revised
body on all four replicas at a median of 1.257--1.761\,s (P95 up to 2.015\,s); both include 250-ms observation
polling. Each normal payment spends 94 FUEL and each compensation adds 5, and
rewards, burn, and refunds sum to the fee caps with zero Held.

Ledger-level regression covers arrival orders directly: all 24 arrival orders
of a four-hop chain, with and without due compensation, under three issuer
sequences give 144 trajectories, and 48 split--merge trajectories vary all
arrival orders of a 40/60 split, two branches, and their merge under two
compensation modes. Every step in the 192 trajectories conserves the
independently computed asset projection. Every trajectory ends with each
issuer's reserve restored, Spent and Reserved zero, $\mathsf{Paid}=\mathsf{Rec}$,
and the consumed inputs still consumed.

Two fourteen-service controls separate permanent loss from delayed
repayment. When the parent never arrives, the grandchild keeps its 100 CAL
and the responsible issuer's reserve stays 100 CAL lower. When a
cross-organization chain arrives grandchild first, the intermediate source
closes its own direct obligation, and the root issuer's compensated 100 CAL
is repaid when the root later executes. The four committee states agree in
both cases, and an absent parent's own fee and work reservations can remain
outstanding.

Three paired
fourteen-service runs on the initial baseline build, before the batch-result
tag separation (4,000 payments at 100 payments/s each), end with identical
owner holdings and reserve balances in normal and withheld-source runs,
consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:security-neutrality}. Concurrent compensation
raises receipt P95 by a factor of 1.98--2.21, and the pairs do not isolate
the responsible stage.

A storage control with NoSync wallets isolates member
commits: receipt P50 is 1.0\,ms with NoSync members and 43--52\,ms with
synchronous commits.

An earlier-build capital series with one run per
condition and no compensation shows that external additions of
10,800--19,700\,CAL sustain 300-s runs of 6,000 two-hop units from a
3,600-CAL start, whereas a fixed 3,600-CAL control closes 46 of 6,000 units.

\subsection{Resubmission, Decisions, and Representation}
\label{sec:eval-repair}

We test whether original signers can deliver a withheld source, whether
compensation and repayment proceed with adaptation unavailable, and what
these mechanisms cost on the ordinary payment path.

\noindent\textbf{Setup.}
The decision-first build runs on the fourteen-service configuration with fresh genesis and
450 initial outputs per round, a disk-backed committee, members in their
default NoSync mode, and flush/gossip settings of 10\,ms. Users pay FUEL, and
every signature, amount, and consumption check remains enabled. The nine
functional runs use the initial implementation snapshot, and the ordinary-path
runs below use the final snapshot (supplement). A functional round certifies a
parent payment, prevents its original publisher from submitting it or running INSTALL
for it once the full QC exists, and has a child spend the parent's TXCer. The
original signers hold their approvals but not the full QC. Three treatments
of three rounds each are run: \emph{resubmission} (the build as designed),
\emph{control} (automatic resubmission disabled), and \emph{paused}
(resubmission disabled and the adaptation endpoints of all four committee
members suspended by a marker, while consensus, decisions, and ordinary
payments continue). Times start at the child's wallet HTTP send. The fast
receipt ends at wallet verification and local recording, and public events are
client observations with polling. These two-hop demonstrations use the default
wallet mode, so their receipt times do not compare with the NoSync stress runs
below.

\noindent\textbf{Resubmission from public certificates.}
In all three rounds the child's fast receipt takes 13.91--13.94\,ms. The
child's block is observed at 1.414, 1.990, and 1.440\,s, and the withheld
source becomes publicly executed at 2.014, 2.565, and 2.039\,s, 575--600\,ms
after the child, with no submission of the original payment by the driver
(Fig.~\ref{fig:c3-resolution}a). Each audit shows one fulfilled obligation, no
compensation, and the issuer's reserve unspent. The recipient can continue
after about 14\,ms, while the roughly 2\,s is the observed completion of the
background source. These intervals are client observations at polling
granularity, not protocol latency bounds, and the 575--600\,ms between the two
public events is not decomposed.

\noindent\textbf{Compensation without adaptation.}
With resubmission disabled, the source stays absent and the obligation
passes its 30-s deadline in every round. The subsequent decision executes
while the obligation remains Open. With adaptation available,
compensation is observed at 35.839, 35.888, and 35.864\,s. The driver then
submits the late source, and the issuer's 100\,CAL are repaid. The 35.8\,s
covers the 30-s obligation period, public-clock advance, background
scheduling, and polling, not cryptographic work. The representation is
materialized 1.13--1.16\,s after compensation (36.994, 37.021, and
37.019\,s).

With all four adaptation services paused, compensation is observed
at 35.837, 35.814, and 35.891\,s (Fig.~\ref{fig:c3-resolution}b). The driver
again submits the late source, and the script lifts the pause only after all
four committee states show the obligation repaid and the representation
neither committed nor materialized. The representation then commits and
materializes, observed at 42.748, 42.736, and 42.798\,s, which includes the
interval for which the pause was held.

The control isolates the resubmission
step and the pause isolates the adaptation dependency. The observed ordering establishes that economic closure and repayment
proceed independently of adaptation in the decision-first build.

In all six control and paused rounds the issuer pays 100\,CAL and is repaid
100\,CAL, no user output is added, and the reserve is restored. The
representation after the pause neither debits nor credits again. The 24
committee observations (six rounds, four members) confirm that the
representation committed and materialized, the body changed, and the identity
checks held; the complete block identity is verified by the materialization
check and the consensus integration tests (supplement). All nine
functional runs pass the audits (equal state hashes at the four committee
members, zero gap, closed payments, drained outboxes), and the resubmission
and control treatments each also carry three runs of 300 ordinary payments, all
completed.

\noindent\textbf{Ordinary-path cost.}
Six runs compare the build at the branch base (commit \texttt{0e7bae5}) with
the final decision-first snapshot in the order AB, BA, AB. Each sends 300
payments at 100 payments/s with 64 concurrent fast requests and 256
outstanding. Taking the median of the per-run statistics, receipt P50 is
1.096\,ms before and 1.071\,ms after, receipt P95 is 27.441 and 30.530\,ms,
block-observed P50 is 1,101 and 1,104\,ms, and completion including all public
confirmations and member following is 3.815 and 3.845\,s. The per-run ranges
overlap for P50 (1.052--1.112 versus 1.045--1.073\,ms), P95 (17.951--39.945
versus 22.084--33.744\,ms), and completion (3.805--3.871 versus
3.791--4.000\,s), and all 1,800 payments complete. These are ordinary-path measurements under short offered loads. They establish neither zero overhead nor
a throughput limit, and ordinary final outputs carry no input certificates, so
they do not exercise the storage of retained certificates for many pending
payments.

\noindent\textbf{Representation batching.}
Structural microbenchmarks of the representation command on the baseline
lineage (Windows amd64, Go~1.27.1) place $k\in\{1,2,4,8\}$ missing inputs in
one historical block and part. Across five repeats, single commands require
$k$ commands and $k$ part adaptations, whereas a batch uses one of each, and
input adaptations remain $k$. At $k=8$, total encoded command bytes fall from
127,336 to 2,752. These counts come from the earlier coupled commands, which
also carried the economic step. The decision-first build adds an 88-byte
decision command per decided output that they exclude, so they do not give
the current protocol's total command traffic. A dispersed control with eight
independent target blocks still needs eight commands and eight part
adaptations while bytes fall from 30,008 to 5,440, so compact fields explain
part of the reduction and shared block representation explains the reduced
adaptation count. These are encoded command sizes, not network traffic or
cryptographic work; the supplement has the figure and an archived
batch-integration run.

A regression with real threshold RSA applies one decided set of 1, 2, 4, and
8 inputs, spanning two real parts, from the original block as one batch, as
single items in order, and in reversed groups of one, two, and three. Each
ends with the same final body and part openings while command histories and
revision numbers differ. This is a finite check of the convergence property,
not a network measurement.

\subsection{Continuation Across Registered Organizations}
\label{sec:eval-network}

To measure the effect of communication distance, we use a two-organization
suite on an archived build recorded in the supplement. Its 24 runs complete
342,000 payments, with two wallet processes holding 32
identities each across 64 lanes and each organization supplying 50
payments/s. The latency comparison uses ten-hop same- and
cross-organization workloads at 0, 20, and 100\,ms injected HTTP RTT, three
repetitions per condition. Each proxy direction adds half the RTT, and
intra-organization certification and committee P2P receive no delay
injection.

  \label{fig:network}

For the common first-120-s cohort, the median of three run-level cross-organization
ACK P50s is 1.632, 21.658, and 101.967\,ms, respectively, and same-organization
values span 1.615--1.744\,ms (Fig.~\ref{fig:network}). Cross-organization ACK P50
tracks the injected RTT, with a measured excess of 1.6--2.0\,ms. An ACK includes the
return path, but a recipient may start its successor on acceptance without
waiting for the ACK to reach the previous sender. At 100-ms injected RTT in the cross-organization workload,
three 300-s runs each complete 30,000 payments; pending peaks are 106--110 and
drain without sustained tail growth. The experiment isolates HTTP path delay
within one physical host.

\subsection{Operating Capacity and Service Availability}
\label{sec:eval-operating}

\noindent\textbf{Independent-input throughput (baseline build).}
Three 30,000-payment runs target 2,000 payments/s on the nine-service
in-memory configuration with NoSync wallets and authorized organization-paid
FUEL. Member processes use a 200\% Go GC target and 16 runtime processors. All
payments complete and the four committee states agree. Completion rates
including drain are 1,750.27, 1,820.91, and 1,887.31 payments/s over
15.9--17.1\,s, with receipt P50s of 2.646, 2.842, and 2.824\,ms and P95s of
47.9, 49.1, and 48.0\,ms. The driver recorded 6,551--7,570 payments that had to
wait for a fast-receipt admission slot, and the P95 dispatch lag was
179--229\,ms, so the offered load was not uniform at 2,000 payments/s. These
are short-run completion measurements on the baseline build. They are not
remeasured for the decision-first build, whose cost on ordinary payments is
tested by the six-run comparison above. The supplement identifies the build
and duration of the 150,000-payment series.

On archived builds, redundant certification and evidence replicated by INSTALL
sustain service under the specified failures. Nine runs at 200 payments/s complete
216,000 payments with one member delayed or paused for 30\,s. Gateway-loss
controls in which one member holds a complete copy replicated by INSTALL
show backup submission 2.503--2.508\,s after INSTALL.
Conflict controls produce neither dual conflicting QCs nor repeated
principal/fee effects, while partial approvals remain a capacity cost.

The multi-identity measurements in supplementary Section~S2 report
per-payment CPU and HTTP-payload costs; 2,048 identities share clients.
Historical memory growth is reported separately from backlog. The same
section gives an LND regtest reference, whose completion event,
persistence, and workload concurrency differ from the Next measurements.
