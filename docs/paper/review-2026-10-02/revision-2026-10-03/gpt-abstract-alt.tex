\begin{abstract}
Next enables recipients to continue UTXO payments before their sources
execute publicly, while assigning funded responsibility for missing
inputs. Organizations certify owner-authorized outputs and reserve
capacity before signing. Public execution preserves successor principal
and records an obligation against the source's issuer. Published
certificates let original signers resubmit retained material; an
independent compensation decision debits reserves, and successful late
execution repays the compensating issuer. Under fixed four-member
organizations and a four-validator committee, with at most one static
Byzantine fault in each, we prove unique consumption, funded outstanding
CAL liabilities including unpublished certificates, and CAL conservation.
For finite, acyclic, source-closed, non-conflicting payment sets
successfully executed in both compared histories, delay preserves final
CAL holdings and reserves, excluding fees. Authorized historical representations preserve payment
and complete block identities without repeating economic effects.
On one M4 Max host, synchronous-member 100-hop chains take 4.557\,s
versus 64.476\,s with inter-hop public waits, using NoSync wallets.
Mixed-load trials close 84,192 payments and complete compensation and
repayment with adaptation paused, while recording transient backpressure.
Local-reader experiments quantify layout-dependent gains against
additional storage and maintenance.
\end{abstract}
