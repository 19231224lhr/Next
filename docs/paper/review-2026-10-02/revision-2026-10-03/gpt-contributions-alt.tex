\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C1: Certified continuation before public source execution.}
We separate one-round organization certification and recipient recording
from evidence replication and public submission. Atomic input locking
and capacity reservation precede signing. Direct output certificates
support same- and cross-organization continuation without transferring
the payment principal into a bilateral channel; for fixed input/output
shapes, request evidence does not grow with ancestor depth.

\item \textbf{C2: Funded direct responsibility with explicit closure rules.}
Public consumption of a missing certified input establishes an obligation
against its direct issuer without delaying the successor's principal
effects. Under the stated fixed-membership fault model, we bound
outstanding CAL liabilities including hidden certificates, and prove
unique consumption and CAL conservation. For finite, acyclic,
source-closed, non-conflicting payment sets successfully executed in
both compared histories, final CAL holdings and reserves coincide;
fees and unconditional execution are not covered by this equivalence.

\item \textbf{C3: Evidence-driven resolution and authorized historical
representation.}
Original signers reconstruct and resubmit sources using retained
approvals and publicly exposed input certificates without new authorization.
Independent
compensation decisions separate economic closure from threshold
adaptation. Later commands record the actual reserve debit while
preserving payment authorizations, successor references, and the complete
block identity, with no repeated economic effect. A fixed-prefix local
reader contract and an indexed-history comparison make the additional
representation's interface value, storage, and maintenance costs explicit.
\end{itemize}
