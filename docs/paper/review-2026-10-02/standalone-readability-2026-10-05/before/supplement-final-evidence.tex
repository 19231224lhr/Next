\noindent\textbf{Premises and evidence levels.}
The proofs cover finite traces under the stated model; production
guards identify implementation responsibilities, and regressions
exercise particular acceptance and rejection traces.
Fixed four-member organizations and a four-validator committee,
three-vote quorums, at most one static Byzantine participant per group,
consistent configuration, and non-rollback of honest signing state remain
model or deployment premises. Aggregate genesis backing and protected-CAL
restrictions map to \texttt{Engine.NewEngine}, the fixed dispatcher,
and the account guards above.
Signature unforgeability, binding original commitments, and correctly
generated dealer-based threshold RSA parameters are not established by
functional tests; fixed-target opening convergence additionally requires
unique valid openings.
Historical reads trust one verified, executed prefix $V_H$ with retained
history, while progress retains its capacity, resource, and fair-delivery
conditions.
The new \texttt{TestPublicAuthorization\allowbreak AtABCIEntry} sends already encoded,
tampered authorization bytes directly through the three ABCI interfaces
and checks rejection without business-state changes.
Sender-side encoding failures are separate; receiver-side decoding,
binding, and signature-verification rejections are distinct outcomes.
This test does not identify the deepest rejection stage for each case;
encoding or decoding rejection is not evidence that signature verification executed.
Existing cache regressions distinguish byte-identical reuse from
current-ledger validation.
The current eight-package regression passes, with client and test
additions fingerprinted separately from the production baseline.
Comet-interface, persistent-restart, network-evidence, and threshold
tests retain their recorded versions and scope; they are not new
remote-download experiments, exhaustive Byzantine-scheduling tests,
or mechanized proofs.

\subsection{Recovery and Boundary Evidence}
\paragraph{Automatic source resubmission (R).}
On the frozen build, one run schedules 2,000 ordinary payments (100/s for 20\,s)
plus three cross-organization parent--child pairs, 2,006 payments in total, with
automatic resubmission enabled and adaptation not paused. For each pair, the driver
obtains the parent's three signatures through a collector that neither persists,
installs nor submits it, and the driver never submits the parent itself; the client
stays alive and observes, so this is withholding, not process-crash injection. The
child spends the parent's TXCer. After the child's public execution exposes that
certificate, the parent's original signers rebuild and submit the parent. All three
obligations end Fulfilled and none is compensated; all 2,006 payments close,
committee state hashes agree, outboxes are empty, and member CAL, execution and
byte-budget reservations return to zero. The three pairs are three recovery targets,
not repetitions. Times are client observations; outbox creation times were not
recorded.

\paragraph{Rotating-signer bound.}
A deterministic regression uses production approvals, public execution and
compensation. One Byzantine member signs without applying its local limit; the three
honest members co-sign in the pairs $(0,1)$, $(0,2)$, $(1,2)$. Each round certifies a
100-CAL source that loses on global Intent; a valid child then consumes its output and
the issuer reserve is debited. With $B_g=300$ the reserve falls $300\to200\to100\to0$,
honest reservations are $(100,100,0)$, $(200,100,100)$, $(200,200,200)$, and the fourth
request is refused by all three honest members. A repeated decision changes nothing and
CAL/FUEL are conserved. The run attains $Q_g=B_g$, so the two-round stop in
Table~\ref{tab:s3-ledger} is specific to a fixed signer set. It measures neither attacker profit nor
cost: the source inputs stay locked and FUEL is spent.

\paragraph{Version bridge.}
Between 721800c and 9879f64, five member production files changed
(\texttt{blocks.go}, \texttt{direct.go}, \texttt{outcome.go}, \texttt{partial\_reclaim.go},
\texttt{source\_recovery.go}); the 721800c N/R/C/P and capital results therefore keep their
original build and are not inherited. Between 9879f64 and f856c7b, the checked member,
committee, rules, redaction, transport, wallet, protocol, finality, node entry points, dependencies and Comet production
files are unchanged; only client and test files differ. The three redaction core files and
the listed Comet overlay files have identical SHA-256 digests in all three builds. No
older number is relabelled.

\paragraph{Retries in wait mode.}
Wait mode uses 414 attempts for 300 payments (140, 138 and 136 per run). The client
sleeps 50\,ms after every failed attempt, whatever the cause (transport, HTTP status or
read error), and the original logs do not classify failures. The 114 extra attempts
therefore include 5.7\,s of fixed sleep over the three runs, plus unmeasured
service time. We report the $13.70\times$ ratio with retries included and derive no
retry-free figure.

\paragraph{Manifest scope.}
The manifest has 309 entries: 305 build-source files, two experiment runners,
and \texttt{go.mod} and \texttt{go.sum}.
Chain, P, and R node binaries, the client, and expanded Comet sources match exactly.
The \texttt{member} wrapper executes \texttt{member-real}.
It sets \texttt{GOMAXPROCS} to 16 and \texttt{GOGC} to 200.

\paragraph{Dispatch-slot interpretation.}
The driver acquires a fast slot before serialized pacing and releases it after
certified receipt. In P run 3, 63.31\% of aggregate slot time precedes HTTP send;
its P95 is 589.422\,ms, compared with 589.418\,ms of recorded pacing wait.
At the 917 fast-limit checks, timestamp reconstruction gives medians of 59
acquired-but-unsent tasks and five sent tasks awaiting receipt.
The reconstruction uses the receipt timestamp immediately before slot release.
Thus a fast-limit hit does not identify a slow member request. Total unfinished-task
backpressure remains in the results; no runtime trace was collected to attribute
its underlying service delay to CPU scheduling, storage, or block following.
