Consider a local archival review that keeps each block's original commit
evidence and transaction coordinates. After compensation, its input review
must associate a reserve advance with the payment that consumed the missing
output. An index or a materialized funding view supplies that association
as an annotation to the unchanged original body. C3 offers a different
record format: the designated input field itself names the historical
reserve debit, and the authorized body remains compatible with the saved
commit. This lets the review replace its body representation while retaining
the block record and its existing commit evidence.

The consumer reads the authorized body and exact Revision and Task from
$V_H$, reconstructs the block and parts with that revision's openings,
and checks the complete BlockID against the saved commit through Next's
verification interfaces. Votes sign both the block hash and the part-set
header. Since the funding slot is outside the fixed fields hashed for
consensus transactions, matching the header alone does not establish
part-set compatibility. The consumer then extracts the reserve-debit
reference at $(h,j,k)$ and associates it with the permanent decision and
obligation state at $H$. The old commit establishes identity compatibility;
successful execution of the exact Task authorizes the new bytes.

Our prototype exercises this workflow, including late repayment.
Table~\ref{tab:c3-function} distinguishes its body-level requirement from
an economic-state query, for which the index and ordinary view suffice.
This is a Next-aware local consumer, not an unmodified CometBFT client.
``Optional'' means that economic closure proceeds with the subsequent
representation service stopped; the current build still uses the CH
transaction and part encodings.
