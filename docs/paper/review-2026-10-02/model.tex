\subsection{Participants, Assets, and Trust}
\label{sec:model-participants}
Next operates a native UTXO ledger. CAL carries payment value, while FUEL pays processing fees. Users spend their own outputs; organizations provide additional CAL in public reserve accounts. Access to the fast path requires an output with a supported organization route, rather than depositing payment principal into a guarantor's reserve or opening a bilateral channel. The default fee source is the user's final FUEL outputs; an explicitly authorized organization-funded mode is also supported.

Each organization has four fixed members and a replaceable collector. Members independently validate requests and authorize payments. The collector distributes requests, assembles certificates, and coordinates propagation without independent spending authority. A separate committee $\mathcal C$ has four equally weighted validators, with three votes required for commitment. It orders public commands and maintains accounts, outputs, obligations, and repair authorizations.

The adversary controls at most one member per organization and one committee validator throughout an execution. Corruption is static; users and collectors may behave arbitrarily. Honest participants share a trusted network configuration, genesis, rules, and organization registry. Each organization route identifies one active configuration and one consumption-lock domain. Owner and member signatures are unforgeable; canonical digests and original block commitments satisfy their required binding properties. Repair uses threshold adaptation, while permission to apply a repair comes from public validation and commitment. Threshold material is generated and distributed by a trusted offline dealer.

Honest signing state is atomic and neither forgotten nor rolled back. Public execution is deterministic and atomically committed; followers authenticate consecutive public prefixes. Message delivery need not be timely for safety. Progress requires sufficient responsive participants, available evidence, fees and resources, and fair delivery and scheduling. The experimental in-memory configuration provides running-state atomicity, with each run starting from fresh genesis.

\subsection{Outputs, Routes, and Certificates}
\label{sec:model-certificates}
An output specifies its asset, amount, owner, and consumption route. Its identifier $x$ is derived from the network, creating transaction, and output position. Spending is indexed by $(x,i)$, where ordinary outputs use instance $i=0$. Every input of a fast payment is assigned to the same consumption organization, and its owner authorizes the new transaction. Newly created outputs may select another registered organization. Thus, cross-organization continuation changes the route of the new output without transferring the previous issuer's responsibility.

A TXCer combines a summary with a quorum certificate (QC): three distinct member signatures over the same fact $c_T$. The summary binds the network, transaction identity, issuer, configuration, epoch, rules, output commitments and amounts, and admission grants. Output bodies may be delivered separately; the recipient checks the corresponding commitment, position, and ownership before saving an output. The certificate authenticates the issuer's guarantee; it is neither an additional asset nor an already registered public obligation.

We distinguish \emph{certified receipt}, when the wallet verifies and locally records an output, \emph{public execution}, when a committed command consumes inputs and creates outputs, and \emph{liability closure}, when a missing source is fulfilled or compensated. Receipt supplies evidence for a subsequent request; that request undergoes its own authorization and resource checks. The implementation's \texttt{Settled} flag denotes successful public execution and may coexist with nonzero \texttt{Pending}. Source arrival below means successful public execution of the source transaction, rather than receipt of its bytes.

\subsection{Three-Layer Design}
\label{sec:model-layers}
\noindent\textbf{Certification and continuation.}
A member atomically reserves the input instances and applicable resources before signing. A CAL grant $g$ authorizes $B_g$; each member receives $\lfloor2B_g/3\rfloor$ signing capacity, partitioned exclusively among its local execution Workers. A payment reserves $a_T$, the sum of all its CAL outputs, including change. These overlapping signing capacities constrain commitments rather than creating additional reserves. The collector returns a TXCer after three matching approvals in one parallel request--response round. Recipient verification and atomic local recording complete the fast path; saving complete relay material, member installation (\texttt{INSTALL}), and public submission proceed in the background. Input locks persist independently of budget release.

\noindent\textbf{Source-decoupled public execution.}
The public command carries the transaction, admission vector, organization authorization, and direct input certificates. The committee reconstructs the certified summary and verifies the authorization without a separate new-output certificate wrapper. It also checks the current consumption state. When a certified input has not yet been publicly created, the committee can atomically record its consumption, register certificate-wide coverage against the issuer, create $\mathcal O_x$ and the corresponding gap, and create the child outputs. Registration covers all outputs of the certificate once; a direct obligation arises only for a missing output actually consumed. Execution uses direct evidence rather than recursively collecting ancestor transaction bodies.

Wallets, members, and collectors independently follow authenticated
blocks and results. Members restore capacity against their own recorded
debits. After observing the successful execution of its own payment, a
member restores its CAL debit down to the net compensation it recorded
for that payment's outputs, and applies only the increase over its
previous restoration. Because a source's execution repays every
compensated output of that payment in the same transition, this net
amount is zero at that point. A reserve loss therefore persists only
while its source remains unexecuted. Genesis checks aggregate grants
against each backing account. Top-ups debit accounts outside the
protected reserve set and atomically increase the destination balance
and grant; authorizations never decrease during an execution.

\noindent\textbf{Compensation, recovery, and historical repair.}
A missing obligation closes through source arrival or, after a deadline
determined by public block time, compensation from its issuer's reserve.
A source that arrives after compensation repays the compensating issuer
within its own execution. A repair commits the economic changes together
with a validated replacement of the designated \texttt{Funding}/opening
and its revision task. Restricted chameleon-hash adaptation preserves the
fixed authorization, output references, and complete block identity
$(H_b,P_b)$. \texttt{RepairBatch} groups obligations targeting one
historical block and applies their economic effects sequentially within
one atomic transaction.

Original command history, committed logical revisions $\rho_\ell$, and installed physical revisions $\rho_p$ remain separate. Installation may advance directly to an authorized later version without repeating compensation. Economic replay uses original commands and subsequent repairs; existing successor payments require neither new signatures nor re-execution.

\subsection{A Continuous-Payment Example and Design Goals}
\label{sec:model-example}
Alice pays Bob 100 CAL through $G_A$, creating output $x$ routed to $G_B$. Bob verifies its TXCer and obtains a certificate for a payment to Carol. If Bob's payment executes first, the committee consumes $(x,0)$, records a 100-CAL obligation against $G_A$, and creates Bob's payment outputs. Carol can continue from this payment without obtaining Alice's transaction body.

If Alice's payment executes successfully before compensation, it closes
the obligation without reviving consumed instance~0. Once public time
reaches the deadline, an obligation that remains Open can instead be
closed by an authorized repair that debits $G_A$ and replaces the funding
reference in Bob's payment; Carol receives no second principal credit. If
Alice's payment executes after compensation, it consumes its own inputs
under the same rules and repays $G_A$ the 100~CAL it advanced. No second
output is created for Bob, whose payment was already funded, and instance
$(x,0)$ stays consumed. If Alice's payment never executes, $G_A$ bears
the loss.

The design targets continuation before public execution, funded direct obligations, and preservation of existing payments across source resolution. It connects the recipient's certified evidence to a bounded public responsibility and an auditable repair path, while keeping historical installation outside the normal payment's waiting conditions.
