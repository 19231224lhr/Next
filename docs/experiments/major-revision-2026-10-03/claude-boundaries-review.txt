可用英文（三段）

Resource retention on the guarantor's side comes in four distinct forms, and we do not conflate them. (i) Fragmentation without a quorum certificate: independent requests, each holding fewer than three approvals, occupy per-member CAL slices; in our fixture four requests with two approvals each leave every member with 200 reserved and 0 available, so any further approval is refused with a capacity error. No certificate exists, no public obligation is created, and the reserve account is untouched. A reserve increase raises each slice (here from 200 to 300 for ΔB=150), and the same requests then complete and release capacity, but it deletes no existing approval. (ii) Same-input 2/2 split: two conflicting authorizations of one input, each approved by two honest members, never reach a quorum, because every member's input lock refuses the other request. This is an input-lock conflict, not a capacity shortage, and additional funding does not lift it.

(iii) Local reclamation after a public winner: when a request that did obtain a certificate loses to a publicly executed winner, the remaining local reservation is returned exactly once while the original approval and the locks on other inputs are retained. (iv) Cross-organization Intent failure with a certificate, followed by payout: the loser holds a valid certificate, its output is accepted downstream under the direct guarantee, the public obligation times out, and a compensation decision pays it from the source organization's reserve. The source input stays unconsumed in the public state but remains locked at the three signers, and the user's FUEL fee input is frozen in full (10,000 in our fixture), separately from the 1,000 maximum fee. In two rounds the reserve fell 300→200→100 while the three signers reached 200 reserved and 0 available; new independent requests were refused at those signers while 100 CAL remained in the reserve. We report this as slice exhaustion preceding reserve exhaustion, not as a reserve-sized bound on service.

Operating condition. Slice-level capacity exhaustion is excluded for a member m and worker w when C_mw ≥ L_mw + N_mw·a_max, where L_mw is residual reservation from other requests, N_mw counts all distinct not-yet-released requests that may occupy the slice (partial approvals, certified but unreleased, and executed but not yet followed), and a_max includes change. In our four-member, three-signature, at-most-one-malicious setting the condition must hold at every honest member's slice (any three if one member is faulty). This is a deployment condition on a controlled workload, not an admission control enforced by the protocol: public collectors can bypass any gateway, so the condition must not be read as a denial-of-service guarantee. It also does not cover FUEL availability, conflict-free authorization, or payment executability, which are separate assumptions. Our experiments use one worker and two rounds, so they illustrate the accounting but do not test multi-worker allocation.
