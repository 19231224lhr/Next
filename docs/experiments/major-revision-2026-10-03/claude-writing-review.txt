\begin{abstract}
Continuous payments let a recipient spend an output before its creating transaction executes publicly. In the Next UTXO ledger, a recipient verifies a quorum-signed output certificate (TXCer) after one certification round and can then request a successor, while replication and public execution proceed in the background. If the successor executes first, the committee consumes the missing input and records a direct obligation against the certificate's issuer. The issuer's original signers can resubmit the source from the certificate that the successor's block publishes. If the source is still absent at its deadline, a public decision debits the issuer's reserve, and a late source repays it. An optional layer of restricted chameleon-hash commands later records the debit in the stored block at the original coordinates, preserving owner authorizations, successor references, and the complete BlockID; economic closure does not wait for it. For four-member organizations and a four-validator committee, each requiring three votes and containing at most one static Byzantine member, we prove unique consumption, funding of every certificate's outstanding CAL liability, and delay-neutral terminal CAL holdings for source-closed, conflict-free payment sets. On one host with synchronous member commits, a 100-hop chain reaches its last certified receipt in a median 4.557\,s, versus 64.476\,s with per-hop waiting.
\end{abstract}

Our insight is that the party answerable for a missing source is already named by the certificate that travels with the output. Next charges the gap to that direct issuer instead of tracing ancestors, bounds every signed certificate's liability by reserve-backed signing capacity, and closes an unresolved obligation by a public decision without changing stored history. The design has three contributions; C2 carries the central technical argument.

\noindent\textbf{C1: A layered architecture for one-round continuation.} Three roles are kept apart. The members of the consuming organization certify an output, the public committee orders and executes commands, and the issuing organization's reserve stands behind the certificate. Members lock inputs and reserve capacity atomically before signing, and one parallel round yields a TXCer. The recipient verifies and records the output and can request a successor at once, carrying only certificates of its direct inputs. Replication and public submission run in the background. The separation is motivated by the payment path and by the placement of responsibility. We do not claim a scalability benefit for it beyond the measurements reported here.

\noindent\textbf{C2: Direct responsibility with source decoupling.} Public execution atomically consumes a missing certified input and records an obligation against its issuer. The successor's block publishes the source's certificate, so the original signers can resubmit the source from stored approvals. After the deadline, a public decision debits the issuer's reserve without threshold adaptation, and a late source repays it. Each approval reserves the full CAL output, change included, while it remains potentially certifiable. An authenticated conflicting public spend may invalidate a non-certified local approval and release only its unused local reservation. For four-member organizations and a four-validator committee with at most one static Byzantine member each, we prove unique consumption and that signing capacity covers the outstanding CAL liability of every certificate, published or not. For a finite, acyclic, conflict-free payment set that contains the sources of its certified inputs and executes each payment once, all orders of arrival and compensation end with the same CAL holdings and reserve balances. Overlapping per-member budgets and budget exhaustion without a quorum certificate have precedents in Stingray~\cite{stingray}. Our additions are responsibility for certificates that were never published, conservation of the dynamic Paid, Recovered, and Remaining quantities, and closure of the source obligation.

\noindent\textbf{C3: Optional authorized historical representation.} Restricted chameleon-hash commands replace the funding reference of a compensated input with the reserve reference, preserving owner authorizations, successor references, and the complete BlockID. An honest replica that has executed prefix $H$ can serve, at the original coordinates $(h,j,k)$, the original and the authorized body bytes together with the complete BlockID, the Revision and Task that authorize those bytes, the historical funding reference, and the current economic state of the obligation. The permanent decision remains the economic basis, and replay of the original commands is unchanged. Representation is off the payment path and costs extra records and local maintenance. We describe it as an optional capability for readers that want the compensated funding source in the stored block. We do not claim that it is required for economic closure or that no alternative layout could serve such readers.

On one M4 Max host with synchronous member commits and unsynchronized wallet stores, a 100-hop chain reaches its last certified receipt in a median 4.557\,s, against 64.476\,s when the same system waits for public execution at each hop. A fourteen-service mixed workload with organization-paid FUEL completes 84,192 payments under bounded concurrency; original signers resolve all 24 sources withheld in the resubmission treatment, and compensation and repayment complete while all adaptation services are paused. In local hot-page reads without object caches, representation lowers point-read P50 by 7.44--12.32\% against an optimized decision index for the tested layout, adding 0.28--2.19\,MiB and one 43--214\,ms maintenance step per repaired block. These read measurements characterize the cost of the optional layer in that configuration.

\subsection{Design Choices}
\label{sec:design-choices}

Next separates three duties that a single mechanism could combine. The consumption organization's four members certify an output after locking its inputs and reserving signing capacity. The public committee orders and executes commands. The issuing organization's reserve funds an obligation that remains open. Certification lets the recipient continue after one round with one organization, and public ordering stays off that path. The duty to answer for an unexecuted source falls on the issuer named in the certificate, not on the ancestors of the payment.

The separation has a cost. A certificate can exist outside the public state, so an accounting based only on public gaps would miss commitments that have been signed but not published. Each member therefore charges its signing capacity before it votes and the proofs bound certificate liability by this capacity. The public collector does not enforce admission or limit concurrent requests. Slice-level capacity is thus a condition on the deployed workload, not a property the protocol imposes on arbitrary submitters. We state this condition with the resource analysis. Throughput or latency gains from the separation at larger scales are a design objective that we do not measure.

\subsection{Historical Read Interface}
\label{sec:c3-interface}

The interface is defined for an honest replica that has executed prefix $H$ and verified the blocks it serves. For coordinates $(h,j,k)$ with $h\le H$, it returns the following items.
\begin{itemize}
\item The original block bytes and the authorized body bytes of height $h$.
\item The complete BlockID, namely the stable header hash together with the original part-set header.
\item The Revision number and the Task identifier that authorize the body.
\item The funding reference of input $k$ of transaction $j$ in the authorized body, which names the reserve debit.
\item The current economic state of the corresponding obligation.
\end{itemize}
A consumer checks that the Task contains exactly the returned body and the matching version, that the parts rebuilt from the body under the original part-set header reproduce the BlockID, and that the owner authorizations, the quorum certificate, and the chameleon hash of the input verify. The historical funding reference and the current obligation state are separate fields. After a late source repays the obligation, the authorized body is unchanged and the economic state reports the recovered amount. We provide a runnable consumer that exports these fields from a local replica and checks them across a late repayment. It runs over a local, already verified replica and gives no remote proof. We know of no deployed client that requires it. Its read cost is the measured layout-specific cost reported in the evaluation.

\subsection{Chameleon-Hash Instantiation}
\label{sec:ch-params}

\noindent\textbf{Parameters.} The hash is an RSA chameleon hash with a 2048-bit modulus $N$ and public exponent $e=65537$. For context $c$ and message $m$, the digest is $\mathrm{CH}=H(c,m)\cdot r^{e}\bmod N$ with randomness $r$ in $\mathbb{Z}_N^{*}$. Given the digest of $(c,m,r)$ and a new message $m'$, the adapted randomness $r'=r\,(H(c,m)/H(c,m'))^{d}\bmod N$ satisfies $H(c,m')\,r'^{e}=H(c,m)\,r^{e}$, where $d=e^{-1}$ modulo the group order. Because $\gcd(e,\varphi(N))=1$ holds for correctly generated keys, $r\mapsto r^e$ is a permutation of $\mathbb{Z}_N^{*}$. Each message and digest then determine a unique valid opening.

\noindent\textbf{Full-domain hash.} $H$ reads a SHAKE256 stream over the prefix \texttt{UTXO\_CH\_RSA\_FDH\_V3}, the 256-byte modulus, $\mathrm{u64be}(|c|)$, the context $c$, $\mathrm{u64be}(|m|)$, and the message $m$. It takes consecutive 256-byte blocks from the stream and returns the first value that is nonzero, below $N$, and a unit modulo $N$. The length prefixes make the encoding injective across contexts and messages. The contexts of block parts depend on the height and the part index and not on the revision counter.

\noindent\textbf{Threshold trapdoor.} A trusted dealer generates the key, splits the exponent into four shares with Cloudflare CIRCL v1.6.5 \texttt{tss/rsa} (\texttt{Deal(4,3,true)}), and the experiments never persist the complete private key. Each share holder signs the public ratio $H(c,m)/H(c,m')$ as a raw group element, not through a padded RSA signing API. \texttt{CombineSignShares} recombines the shares and the combiner tries at most four three-member groups until the final opening verifies against the public relation. The construction follows the threshold RSA approach of Shoup~\cite{shoup2000}. The interface itself is ours, and we do not attribute its security properties to the library.

\noindent\textbf{Assumptions and scope.} CIRCL's public-key constructor checks only that the modulus is a 2048-bit odd number and that $e$ is acceptable. It does not prove that the modulus is well formed, so the argument needs the dealer to generate and split the key correctly. The ratio is public and algebraically combinable, so the chameleon hash does not by itself establish that one authorization was issued for one replacement. An adaptation takes effect only through a committed compensation decision and an exact Task. These are checked independently of the hash. The original body is bound by the original parts at revision~0 with opening~1 together with the complete-identity checks described in Sec.~\ref{sec:c3-interface}. This binding does not depend on secrecy of the trapdoor. Consensus safety still rests on the BFT, authentication, and state guards of the base protocol.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Next separates three paths that continuous payments otherwise serialize. One-round certification lets a recipient spend a newly certified output, so on one host with synchronous member commits a 100-hop chain reaches its last certified receipt in 4.557\,s rather than 64.476\,s. \noindent\textbf{Four kinds of retained signing capacity.} (i) \emph{Fragmentation without a quorum certificate.} Independent requests that each hold only two approvals occupy per-member CAL slices. In our fixture, four such requests leave every member with 200 reserved and 0 available, and further approvals are refused for capacity. No certificate or public obligation exists. A reserve increase of 150 raises each slice without deleting any approval, after which the same requests complete and release their capacity. (ii) \emph{Same-input 2/2 split.} Two conflicting authorizations of one input, each approved by two honest members, produce no certificate, because each member's input lock refuses the other request. Funding does not lift this lock. The per-member reservations and the reserve and public usage are recorded separately from the lock. (iii) \emph{Local reclamation after a public winner.} If a request without a certificate loses to a publicly executed winner on the same input, the unused local reservation of its stale approval is returned exactly once. The original approval and the locks on other inputs remain, and the invalidated fact cannot be revived. (iv) \emph{Cross-organization Intent failure with a certificate, followed by payout.} A request with a quorum certificate that fails the global Intent binding while spending disjoint inputs is not reclaimed. Its certified responsibility, input locks, and signing reservation remain, and the compensation decision debits the reserve. The source input stays unconsumed in the public state but remains locked at the three signers. A user-paid FUEL input is frozen in full (10,000 in the fixture), apart from the 1,000 maximum fee. Over two rounds the reserve fell from 300 to 200 to 100, and the three signers ended at 200 reserved and 0 available, so new independent requests were refused at those signers while the fourth member still had 200 available and the reserve held 100. The second round reused only the 100 CAL that returned to the A side, and the B side used a new initial input each round. We did not measure a cycle of fixed capital on both sides, and we did not test cycling before the deadline.

Slice-level exhaustion is excluded for member $m$ and worker $w$ when $C_{mw}\ge L_{mw}+N_{mw}\,a_{\max}$. Here $L_{mw}$ is residual reservation from other requests and $N_{mw}$ counts every distinct request that may still occupy the slice, whether partially approved, certified but unreleased, or executed but not yet followed. The value $a_{\max}$ includes change. The condition must hold at three honest slices. It describes a controlled workload and not an admission rule, since public collectors can bypass a gateway, and it is no denial-of-service guarantee.
